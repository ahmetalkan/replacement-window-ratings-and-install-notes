Replacement Window Ratings and Installation Notes: Read the NFRC Label

Window quotes are notoriously hard to compare because manufacturers market proprietary glass names rather than plain specs. Every certified replacement window carries an independent National Fenestration Rating Council (NFRC) label that puts thermal efficiency, solar heat control, and daylight on one comparable scorecard.

ENERGY STAR pairs NFRC performance metrics with regional climate-zone targets across the United States—and also reminds homeowners that sealing attic and wall air leaks often delivers faster comfort gains alongside or ahead of window replacement.

Checklist before signing a window contract

Pricing guides by window style and material are compiled on Window Replacement Lab and its window replacement cost guide.

  • Request the exact NFRC CPD number or label printout for each proposed window line and glass package—numbers vary by operator style (casement vs double-hung) and grid options.
  • Check air-leakage (AL) rating: Lower air-infiltration ratings matter noticeably on windy exposures; compression-seal casements generally seal tighter than sliding sashes.
  • Verify bedroom egress dimensions: Pocket inserts shrink the clear glass opening slightly; confirm bedroom windows still meet local emergency-escape opening rules.
  • Confirm lead-safe practices on pre-1978 homes: Disturbing older painted wood frames falls under EPA RRP lead-safe renovation rules when contractors work on pre-1978 housing.
Even a high-performing triple-pane unit leaks drafts and rainwater if sill pans, flashing tape, and low-expansion perimeter foam are skipped during installation.
